# Voice-over pacing

When a film has narration, the voice is the clock: scenes start when sentences start, text appears when words
are spoken, and pauses are where the picture breathes. Pacing the voice-over well is the difference between a
film that feels calm and one that feels rushed.

## The idea

- **Speaking rate**: about 130–160 words a minute for clear narration; under 130 sounds slow, over 160
  rushed for explanation.
- **Pauses are structure**: a short pause (about 0.3–0.5 s) between sentences, a longer one (0.8–1.5 s)
  between sections, gives room for a visual change.
- **Picture follows voice**: a visual appears on the spoken word or a few frames before it, never after.
- **Scratch first**: record a rough read to time the animatic, then replace with the final voice.

## Rules of thumb (30 fps)

| Rate | Words a second | Frames per word |
|---|---|---|
| 130 wpm | 2.17 | 13.8 |
| 145 wpm | 2.42 | 12.4 |
| 160 wpm | 2.67 | 11.3 |

- Script length: 30 s ≈ 65–75 words; 60 s ≈ 130–150 words.
- On-screen keywords appear 0–4 f before the word is heard and hold for their read time.
- Leave 10–20 f before the first word and after the last for breathing room.

## Do and don't

- Do time scenes to the voice's sentences and pauses.
- Do ask the voice artist for pauses where big visual changes happen.
- Don't animate a busy transition under an important sentence.
- Don't put full sentences of narration on screen; use keywords or captions.

## Checklist

- [ ] Script word count fits the length at 130–160 wpm.
- [ ] Every scene change aligns with a sentence or a pause.
- [ ] Keywords appear on or just before their spoken word.
