# Broadcast graphics of the 1980s and 1990s

The decades when television branding became a design discipline. Expensive dedicated graphics machines
put painting and compositing into broadcast studios; music television turned the channel logo into an
ever-changing canvas; public broadcasters built ident systems that were celebrated as art.

## What defined it

- **The flexible identity**: a music channel launched in 1981 with a logo that had no fixed colour or
  texture, so dozens of animators could reinvent it in every ident. The brand was the shape plus the
  attitude, not a fixed artwork.
- **Ident systems**: a channel number or symbol placed in endlessly varied physical situations (in water,
  paint, silk, sparks): one constant element, many surprising contexts.
- **Dedicated graphics hardware**: digital paint and compositing systems with pressure-sensitive pens became
  the broadcast standard for about fifteen years, making painted backgrounds, chrome type and video
  effects possible on air (and very expensive).
- **The look**: chrome and neon type, lens flares, grids receding to the horizon, video feedback, scan lines,
  bold primaries and Memphis-like patterns.

## Why it matters for motion

- Flexible identity is now the default for brands that live in motion: fixed core, variable expression.
- It established the package vocabulary still used today: idents, bumpers, stings, straps.
- The era's aesthetic returns regularly as retro (synthwave, VHS, chrome type).

## Using the influence well

- For a system: fix two or three things (the mark, the sound, the timing) and vary the rest freely.
- For retro looks: limited palettes of neon on dark, horizon grids, chrome gradients, scan lines, with
  modern clean timing underneath, unless the brief wants deliberate roughness.
- Avoid heavy strobes and flicker that the old looks used; today's limits are stricter
  ([photosensitivity](../accessibility/photosensitivity.md)).
