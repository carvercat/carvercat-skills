# Bauhaus and Constructivism

Two movements of the 1920s that still shape graphic design and motion graphics: the German **Bauhaus**
school, which joined art, craft and industry around simple geometric form, and Russian **Constructivism**,
which put dynamic diagonals, bold type and photomontage at the service of a cause.

## What defined them

- **Bauhaus (1919–1933, Weimar, Dessau, Berlin)**: form follows function; circles, squares and triangles as
  a basic vocabulary; primary colours; experiments with lowercase-only geometric alphabets; "typophoto",
  the merging of type and photography; a foundation course that taught colour, material and composition
  from first principles. Its stage workshop explored figures and shapes moving through space.
- **Constructivism (from about 1915, graphic peak in the early 1920s)**: art as construction rather than
  decoration; strong diagonals and wedges; red, black and white; heavy sans-serif type set at angles;
  photomontage; posters and advertising as public communication.
- Both treated the page as a field of forces: elements pull, push and balance each other. That is already
  a way of thinking about motion.

## Why it matters for motion

- The basic-shapes vocabulary is the native language of 2D motion graphics: shapes that grow, rotate and
  combine.
- Diagonals and asymmetric balance create energy even in a still frame; moving along a diagonal amplifies it.
- Photomontage anticipated layered compositing: cut-out photos, type and flat colour in one space.
- The idea of a design *system* (a few elements, strict rules) underlies every brand motion system today.

## Using the influence well

- Limit the palette (red, black, off-white; or primaries) and the shapes (circle, square, triangle, bar).
- Set type on diagonals sparingly and with intent; keep reading lines short.
- Build compositions as balances of weight; animate elements into balance, not just into place.
- Quote the spirit (economy, geometry, energy) rather than copying famous posters.
