# Memphis design

A Milan design collective of the early 1980s that rejected tasteful modernism with loud colour, clashing
patterns, bold geometric shapes and a sense of humour. Its visual language (squiggles, confetti, terrazzo
dots, zigzags, primary shapes on pastel grounds) returned in graphics in the 2010s and is the root of
today's "funky" motion style.

## What defined it

- Bright, clashing colours: pastels next to primaries, black-and-white patterns as accents.
- Simple geometric shapes (circles, triangles, half-moons) combined like toys.
- Surface pattern everywhere: squiggles, dots, stripes, grids.
- Irony and play: objects that look like props, rules broken on purpose.
- A short life as a movement (about 1981 to 1987) and a long afterlife in fashion, graphics and later in
  flat illustration styles.

## Why it matters for motion

- Its shapes are perfect motion actors: they pop, spin, wiggle and stack.
- It licenses exaggeration: big overshoots, tilts and bounces suit it ([exaggeration](../principles/exaggeration.md)).
- Pattern backgrounds give long holds life without competing with text, if kept slow.

## Using the influence well

- Choose four to six colours and stick to them; clash on purpose, not randomly.
- Use offset hard shadows and outlines for a flat-but-bold look.
- Keep text readable: a solid plate behind it, high contrast, no pattern under words.
- Motion: tilts, pops with 15–30% overshoot, wiggles, slow one-way rotation of decorations.
