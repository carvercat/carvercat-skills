# The desktop motion-design era

Until the early 1990s, broadcast-quality graphics needed machines that cost as much as a house. Layer-based
compositing software on personal computers changed that: within a decade, small studios and freelancers
were making title sequences, commercials and channel brands, and "motion design" (or "mograph") became a
profession of its own.

## The arc

- **Early 1990s**: the first desktop compositing applications appear: layers, keyframes, masks, a handful of
  effects. Early versions were very limited, yet they put the whole craft on one computer.
- **Mid-1990s to 2000s**: desktop tools become the industry standard; design-led studios form around title
  design, commercials and music videos; a style of layered 2D, type and texture defines the period.
- **2000s**: 2D and 3D merge; procedural "mograph" tools for cloning and effecting many objects; online
  showcases and blogs build a global community.
- **2010s**: explosive growth with web video, explainers and UI motion; online schools teach the craft;
  design systems publish motion rules.
- **2020s**: real-time and code-driven pipelines, templates and AI assistance; motion everywhere, from apps to
  stage screens.

## What the era established

- The keyframe-and-curve workflow every tool now shares: keys, easing, graph editing, layers, parenting,
  masks.
- The studio process: brief, style frames, animatic, animation, review, delivery
  ([workflow](../workflow/README.md)).
- A shared vocabulary of techniques: track mattes, trim paths, staggered type, parallax, faux 3D, match cuts.
