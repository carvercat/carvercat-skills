# Product launch films

A launch film introduces something new and makes it feel important: a device, an app, a feature, a
collection. The grammar is restraint: one hero, generous space, slow confident moves, very little text, a
clear "available now".

## The idea

- **Three-part arc**: reveal (the hero appears, often partially), detail (close-ups of the features that
  matter), context (the product in use or in the world), then the name and availability.
- **One hero, staged**: clean background, controlled light, the product (or UI) centred or on a third.
- **Sparse type**: a few words per card, big and calm. The product carries the film.
- **Pacing**: slower than social, faster than luxury; the music builds to the name.
- **Software launches** use the same arc with UI: the feature appears, a cursor does the one action that
  matters, the benefit appears as a single line.

## Rules of thumb

- Teaser 15–30 s; launch film 45–90 s; feature cut-downs 10–20 s each.
- Hero reveal: a dramatic-length move (30–60 f), once; supporting moves 12–20 f.
- One feature per beat, 3–5 s each, with one headline and one action.
- End card: name, one line, the call to action, held at least 2 s.
- Avoid copying another company's protected look; borrow the restraint, not the assets.

## Do and don't

- Do reveal the hero in pieces (a silhouette, a detail, a light sweep) before the full view.
- Do let the music build and land the name on a phrase start ([beat sync](../sound-and-timing/beat-sync.md)).
- Don't list every feature; pick the three that change the buyer's mind.
- Don't bounce a premium product; heavy, smooth moves suit it ([weight](../principles/weight.md)).

## Checklist

- [ ] One hero reveal, staged with space around it.
- [ ] Three to five features, one per beat.
- [ ] Name and availability on the end card, held 2 s or more.
- [ ] Cut-downs planned for each platform.
